Pedro works at Alpina (Colombia) in logistics/distribution and has been the primary driver of an ambitious, ongoing development project building a standalone HTML logistics tool called "Rutero 2" (formally "Programación de Rutas y Cargue Alpina"). The conversation covered extensive feature development across multiple sessions, with Pedro providing real operational files, business context, and iterative feedback to progressively refine the tool toward autonomy and competitive differentiation.

The core workflow the tool handles: Pedro's team receives a daily SAP export of credit-client orders (Base SAP), optionally a warehouse separation report (Rutas Fijas) that includes both credit and cash clients, and now also invoices from a partner brand (Don Maíz) that rides on Alpina's delivery trucks. The tool must generate two outputs: (1) Programación Carros — an exact replica of the original Excel macro's pivot table (RUTA → Punto de Venta → orders/value), and (2) Sugerido de Cargue — an innovative optimization layer whose primary goal is saving vehicles through intelligent route compression, respecting capacity/value/distance/stops constraints. Pedro explicitly clarified that saving vehicles is the core of the application, not a secondary feature, and that route splitting (when a route exceeds one vehicle's capacity) should happen automatically, not just be flagged. Key operational context Pedro provided: the CEDI/warehouse is located at Carrera 48 # 17-49 (Avenida Los Industriales), Medellín (near Estación Industriales). He confirmed this is a key "weak point" (talón) of the current app and wants the tool to eventually decide automatically between recharge vs. additional vehicle. He provided the warehouse address as the first of four needed data points (jornada duration, time per stop, and maximum recharges per day still pending). Pedro also introduced Don Maíz as a multibrand/multichannel integration — Don Maíz has no own routing system, so its invoices (Makro, Jumbo, Éxito, Euro, Farmatodo locations) must be matched by store name against Alpina's route catalog and folded onto the same trucks.
